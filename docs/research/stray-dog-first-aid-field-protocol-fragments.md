# Stray Dog Emergency First Aid: Field Protocol Fragments

Raw material pile mined from primary veterinary triage sources, the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act 1960, and Kiran Singh Foundation field logs.

---

Leading word: **Triage-First** — The instinct in street rescue is to rush and hug; the discipline of rescue is scene safety, improvised restraint, and hemodynamic stabilization before transport.

---

A street dog hit by a vehicle does not know you are trying to help. Fear and excruciating pain trigger an involuntary fight-or-flight reflex. Even the gentlest neighborhood indie will bite when a fractured pelvic limb is handled.

---

Scene safety is non-negotiable. Approach laterally at an angle, staying low. Avoid direct, sustained eye contact, which dogs interpret as predatory challenge. Speak in calm, low-frequency tones.

---

The Improvised Field Muzzle:
A 1-meter strip of clean bandage, cotton cloth, or leash. Form a loop, drop it over the bridge of the snout, tighten gently, cross underneath the jaw, and bring both ends behind the ears to tie in a quick-release bow.
CRITICAL EXCEPTION: Never muzzle a dog that is vomiting, bleeding from the mouth, coughing blood, or gasping with respiratory distress.

---

The Field ABC Triage:
- A (Airway): Check oral cavity for regurgitated food, dirt, or blood clots. Gently extend the neck to align the trachea.
- B (Breathing): Count chest wall expansions. Normal resting rate is 15 to 30 breaths per minute. Rapid, shallow breathing (<50/min) indicates thoracic trauma or shock.
- C (Circulation): Check the gums. A healthy capillary refill time (CRT) is under 2 seconds. White or porcelain gums mean severe internal hemorrhage or circulatory collapse. Blue gums mean severe hypoxia.

---

Arterial vs Venous Bleeding:
Pulsatile, bright-red spurting indicates an arterial laceration. Dark-red, continuous oozing indicates venous bleeding.
Do not waste time hunting for tourniquets. Apply direct, uninterrupted pressure with sterile gauze pads or a clean cloth for a full 5 minutes by the clock. Do not lift the pad to "check" if clotting has begun—lifting breaks the nascent fibrin mesh.

---

The Maggot Wound (Myiasis) Protocol:
Maggot infestations in tropical climates can liquefy deep tissue within 48 hours.
Never pluck live larvae with forceps without first suffocating them; burrowed maggots retreat deeper into muscle fascial planes.
Field debridement: Place cotton plugs saturated with Turpentine oil or Negasunt/Topicure into the cavity for 3 minutes. The larvae suffocate and evacuate outward. Flush thoroughly with dilute Povidone-Iodine (1:10 dilution in saline). Dust with sterile antibiotic powder.

---

Suspected Spinal or Pelvic Fractures:
Never lift an injured street dog under the armpits or belly like a puppy. Lifting by the midsection can displace fractured vertebrae and sever the spinal cord, turning a temporary paraparesis into permanent paralysis.
Use a firm flat surface: a cardboard carton flap, a wooden board, or a taut blanket held by two people at four corners. Slide the dog horizontally onto the stretcher.

---

The Fatal Home Remedy Errors:
- Never give Paracetamol (Crocin), Ibuprofen, or Diclofenac. Human NSAIDs cause catastrophic gastrointestinal ulceration, Heinz body anemia, and acute kidney failure in canines.
- Never force water or milk down the throat of an unconscious, semi-conscious, or recumbent animal. The liquid enters the trachea, causing instantaneous asphyxiation or lethal aspiration pneumonia.
- Never apply raw ice directly to heatstroke victims. Pour cool tap water over the footpads, inner thighs, and groin.

---

Legal Grounding in India:
- Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (PCA) Act, 1960 (Section 11): Injuring or abandoning a distressed animal is a cognizable violation.
- Indian Penal Code (IPC) Sections 428 and 429: Punitive protection against killing or maiming community animals.
- Article 51A(g) of the Constitution of India: Fundamental duty of every citizen to have compassion for living creatures.
- Animal Birth Control (ABC) Rules 2023: Community feeding and treatment rights protected by high court jurisprudence.
