# Stray Dog First Aid: Complete Field Protocol | KSF Dispatch

> **Direct Answer Box (GEO Snippet):**  
> The emergency first aid protocol for an injured stray dog in India follows four sequential phases: secure scene safety using an improvised gauze muzzle, perform the ABC triage (Airway, Breathing, Circulation), apply 5 minutes of direct pressure to arrest bleeding, and immobilize spinal fractures on a rigid board before transport to a veterinarian.

---

### Section 1: Scene Safety & The Defensive Pain Reflex

A street dog hit by a speeding vehicle does not know you are trying to help. Extreme physical trauma triggers an immediate survival reflex. Even the gentlest community dog will bite defensively when in agony.

Before touching the animal, assess traffic and pack dynamics. Look for oncoming cars, agitated bystander dogs, or fallen live wires. Never rush headlong into the road without checking your own perimeter.

Approach the injured animal laterally at a 45-degree angle. Never approach head-on with direct eye contact. Sustained eye contact is perceived as a predator challenge and escalates defensive aggression.

Crouch down slowly to minimize your physical profile. Speak in calm, low-frequency tones. Allow the dog to track your movement without making sudden reaching gestures toward injured limbs.

Prepare an improvised gauze muzzle before lifting the dog. A one-meter strip of clean cotton bandage, clean cloth, or a soft leash is sufficient to secure the jaws safely in the field.

Form an overhand loop in the center of the fabric. Lower the loop over the bridge of the snout from behind the dog's head. Pull it snug around the upper muzzle, but avoid overtightening the nostrils.

Cross the ends beneath the lower jaw. Bring both ends backward beneath the ears to the nape of the neck. Tie them firmly in a quick-release slip bow that can be untied in one second.

> [!WARNING]
> **Field Safety Exception:** Never muzzle a dog that is vomiting, bleeding from the mouth, coughing blood, or suffering acute respiratory gasping. An obstructed airway during vomiting causes lethal choking.

### Section 2: Rapid Diagnostic ABC Triage Sequence

Every seconds-count field emergency requires systematic evaluation. Follow the clinical ABC triad to isolate life-threatening collapses before tending to visible surface wounds.

| Triage Step | Clinical Parameter | Healthy Benchmark | Emergency Warning Indicator |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A (Airway)** | Oral cavity clearance | Clear airflow, pink tongue | Foreign objects, vomit, vocal gurgling |
| **B (Breathing)** | Thoracic wall expansion | 15–30 breaths per minute | Rapid panting (>50/min), blue gums |
| **C (Circulation)**| Capillary Refill Time | Under 2.0 seconds | White/pale gums, undetectable pulse |

#### Phase A: Airway Management
Inspect the oral cavity immediately after securing restraint. Check for regurgitated food, road dirt, dislodged teeth, or thick blood clots blocking the pharynx.

Gently pull the tongue forward if the dog is unconscious. Clearing the tongue opens the larynx and prevents backward airway collapse. Align the head and neck in a straight, neutral line.

#### Phase B: Breathing Assessment
Observe the rhythmic rise and fall of the ribcage. A normal resting canine breathes 15 to 30 times per minute. Rapid, shallow gasping indicates internal pulmonary contusions or shock.

Look for paradoxical chest movement where one side moves inward during inhalation. This signals broken ribs or pneumothorax, requiring immediate supplemental oxygen at a veterinary hospital.

#### Phase C: Circulation & Capillary Refill Time (CRT)
Check the perfusion of vital organs by pressing a finger firmly against the pink upper gum line for one second. Release your finger and count the seconds until the pink color returns.

A healthy capillary refill time is under two seconds. If the gums remain white or pale gray for more than two seconds, the animal is suffering acute hypovolemic shock or massive internal hemorrhage.

Porcelain white gums accompanied by hypothermia demand urgent intravenous fluid therapy. Brick-red gums signal systemic sepsis, while bluish or purplish gums indicate severe oxygen deprivation.

### Section 3: Severe Hemorrhage & Wound Management

Uncontrolled hemorrhage is the leading preventable cause of field mortality following road collisions. Differentiate between arterial spurting and venous oozing immediately.

Arterial bleeding appears bright red and spurts in rhythm with cardiac contractions. Venous bleeding is dark crimson and flows in a continuous, non-pulsatile stream from the wound bed.

Apply direct, uninterrupted pressure over the wound using sterile cotton gauze pads or a clean cotton towel. Press firmly with your palm directly against the laceration site for a full five minutes.

Do not lift the dressing to inspect the wound during this window. Lifting the pad tears away the nascent fibrin mesh and restarts active clotting from zero. Keep pressure steady and continuous.

If blood soaks completely through the initial gauze dressing, do not remove it. Add fresh layers of absorbent cotton padding directly on top of the saturated dressing and maintain firm compression.

Wrap an elastic conforming bandage over the pads to maintain continuous pressure. Ensure the bandage is snug enough to arrest oozing without cutting off peripheral blood circulation to the paws.

Check the lower paw pads twenty minutes after bandaging. If the foot becomes swollen, cold to the touch, or cyanotic, loosen the bandage slightly to restore arterial perfusion.

Avoid arterial tourniquets on limbs unless catastrophic arterial spurting cannot be controlled by direct pressure. Prolonged tourniquet ischemia exceeding twenty minutes causes irreversible limb necrosis.

### Section 4: Maggot Wound (Myiasis) Field Care Protocol

In tropical climates, flies lay eggs in superficial scratches within hours. Hatched maggots secrete liquefactive enzymes that consume subcutaneous tissue, burrowing into deep muscle fascia within two days.

Never attempt to extract burrowed maggots individually with forceps without prior suffocation. Agitated larvae retreat deeper into internal crevices when touched, accelerating tissue destruction.

Soak clean cotton balls in pure Turpentine oil or Topicure spray. Place the saturated plugs directly into the opening of the wound cavity. Leave the plugs sealed inside for three to five minutes.

The volatile hydrocarbon fumes deprive the larvae of oxygen. Asphyxiated maggots will immediately abandon deep tissue pockets and crawl outward to the surface of the dressing.

Use blunt forceps to sweep the dead larvae into a disposable container. Repeat the turpentine plug application if deep larvae remain active. Ensure all crevices are systematically cleared.

Flush the emptied wound cavity generously with dilute Povidone-Iodine solution mixed one-to-ten with sterile normal saline. Avoid undiluted hydrogen peroxide, which damages healthy granulation tissue.

Dust the debrided cavity with Neosporin or pure sterile zinc oxide antibiotic powder. Coat the outer skin margins with repellent ointment to prevent secondary fly strikes before professional veterinary review.

### Section 5: Fracture & Spinal Cord Stabilization

A dog hit by a motor vehicle frequently suffers pelvic, femoral, or vertebral trauma. Incorrect handling during transport can sever an intact spinal cord and cause permanent paralysis.

Never pick up an injured street dog by scooping your hands under its abdomen and chest. Sagging of the lumbar spine stretches damaged vertebrae, turning a manageable fracture into permanent paraplegia.

Use a rigid flat stretcher for transport. A sturdy sheet of corrugated cardboard, a flat wooden plywood plank, or a taut wool blanket held firmly by two people at all four corners works effectively.

Place the stretcher flat on the ground alongside the dog. Gently slide the dog horizontally onto the flat surface, keeping the head, spine, and pelvis aligned as a single rigid unit.

Secure the dog to the board using wide cloth bands or soft towels wrapped across the upper chest and hips. Avoid placing tie-down straps directly over the abdomen or across fractured limbs.

Keep the dog warm during vehicular transport. Traumatic shock triggers hypothermia even in warm weather. Drape a dry blanket over the animal, leaving the nostrils and muzzle unobstructed.

### Section 6: Lethal Home Remedies to Avoid

Well-intentioned citizens often cause irreversible toxicity by administering human medicines. A dog's liver cannot metabolize human analgesics, turning household remedies into lethal poisons.

| Substance | Common Trade Brand | Canine Toxic Consequence | Safe Veterinary Replacement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Paracetamol** | Crocin, Dolo, Calpol | Acute liver failure, Heinz body anemia | Meloxicam (Vet prescription only) |
| **Ibuprofen** | Brufen, Combiflam | Perforating gastric ulcers, renal shutdown | Carprofen (Vet prescription only) |
| **Diclofenac** | Voveran | Severe gastrointestinal necrosis | Tramadol (Vet prescription only) |
| **Cow Milk / Water** | Kitchen liquid | Lethal pulmonary aspiration in recumbency | Oral rehydration only when alert |

Never give Paracetamol (Crocin) or Ibuprofen to an injured dog. A single 500mg tablet causes severe toxic hepatitis, respiratory collapse, and fatal kidney damage within twenty-four hours.

Never force liquids, water, or milk down the throat of an unconscious or recumbent dog. Paralyzed swallowing reflexes allow fluid to enter the trachea, causing instantaneous drowning or lethal pneumonia.

Never apply raw ice cubes directly to an animal suffering heatstroke. Freezing cold induces immediate cutaneous vasoconstriction, trapping lethal metabolic heat inside the core organs.

Pour room-temperature tap water gently over the dog's paws, abdomen, and groin. Place wet towels on the head and direct an electric fan across the body until rectal temperature drops below 103°F.

### Section 7: Legal Mandate & Emergency Field Logistics

Assisting an injured community animal is protected by Indian law. Citizens have a recognized constitutional right to provide medical relief without interference from resident welfare associations.

Article 51A(g) of the Constitution of India enshrines the fundamental duty of every citizen to show compassion to living creatures. This constitutional mandate overrides arbitrary local society bylaws.

Section 11 of the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (PCA) Act 1960 establishes that abandoning an injured animal or failing to render medical assistance is a cognizable legal transgression.

Sections 428 and 429 of the Indian Penal Code prescribe imprisonment and fines for anyone who causes bodily harm, poisons, or maims community dogs within neighborhood territories.

The Animal Birth Control (ABC) Rules 2023 reaffirm that street dogs are lawful community residents with legal protection to remain within their native territory following medical treatment.

Document the accident scene thoroughly before transport. Record the vehicle registration number, time, and precise GPS coordinates if the injury resulted from a reckless hit-and-run incident.
